Details: what the columns mean
  • Dollar prices: share of price rows given as a dollar amount. Higher is better.
  • % of charges: share of rows given as a percent of the hospital's billed charges, so there is no dollar price to compare.
  • $0 rows and Duplicate rows: rows with a price of $0, and rows that repeat an earlier one.
  • Key services found: how many of 9 common services have a dollar price.
  • Format checks passed: checks of the file's structure and values (required fields, date format, valid codes). It does not judge whether prices are accurate.

Method

How to read the prices

  • Negotiated rate: the amount an insurer has agreed to pay a hospital for a service. It is not your bill. Your cost depends on your deductible, copay or coinsurance, and on whether the hospital is in your plan’s network.
  • Facility and professional fees: a hospital stay or procedure is often billed twice, once for the hospital’s facility fee (“institutional”) and once for the doctor’s professional fee. Use the billing-type buttons so you compare like with like.
  • Colors on the price lines: green is a lower price, blue a middle price and red a higher price, compared with the median for this hospital (or, on a service page, for all hospitals).
  • Where the prices come from: the in-network rate data that insurers must publish under the federal Transparency in Coverage rule, downloaded and stored locally, then rebuilt about every quarter. Nothing is estimated or filled in.
  • Hospitals: the hospital list is from CMS Care Compare. A hospital appears only if its tax IDs or NPIs can be found in an insurer’s data. Rates are filed under tax IDs, which can also cover clinics and doctors who bill under the hospital.
  • Insurer data versus hospital price lists: hospitals publish their own price lists with a charge for each insurer. Those can differ from the rates the insurers publish, because insurers file by tax ID (including physicians who bill under a hospital) and use different billing codes. This tool shows the insurer side only.
  • What is missing: most UnitedHealthcare plans (one small plan is included) and the largest Anthem plans are not included yet, hospital cash and gross prices are not shown, and a hospital with no dollar price for a service simply has no dot.

What the app measures

  • Dollar share: price rows with a dollar rate above zero (fee schedule, negotiated, derived or per diem) divided by all price rows. A plan is marked sufficient at 50% or more. This borrows the "pricing data sufficiency" idea Patient Rights Advocate uses for hospital data, where over half of negotiated charges must be dollars and cents.
  • Percentage share: rows expressed as a percentage of billed charges. They cannot be compared across insurers without charge data, so they are left out of all price statistics.
  • Zero-dollar rows: rates at or below zero. Marked good at 0%, caution under 1%, poor above.
  • Repeated rows: share of price rows whose code, rate and billing class already appeared earlier in the same plan. High values point to the duplication that makes these plans large. Informational only.
  • Key services: how many of the nine services (knee replacement, C-section, brain MRI, abdomen/pelvis CT with and without contrast, upper GI endoscopy with biopsy, laparoscopic cholecystectomy, metabolic panel, lipid panel) have at least one dollar rate. Good at 7 or more, caution at 4 to 6.
  • Schema checks: structure and value checks against the in-network rate format required by the currently enforced rule (45 CFR 147.211 and the parallel Labor and Treasury rules), using the CMS v2.x JSON schema. Good when all pass, caution when two or fewer fail.
  • Age: days since the plan's last_updated_on. Marked caution after 190 days (Colorado asks for state plans every six months).

Why Colorado plans are smaller

From the Division of Insurance's technical guide (GitHub):

  • Only plans issued or delivered in Colorado, and only providers whose group or billing NPI has a Colorado zip code.
  • In-network rates only for procedure codes billed by a tax ID (TIN) with at least 20 services in the last year.
  • Provider data must be inline in each plan; separate provider sub-plans are not allowed except through the table of contents. A shared provider reference list is optional.
  • Insurers refresh the plans each January 1 and July 1, post a Colorado-specific page with no login or expiring links, and email the Division a list of URLs.
  • Insurers with fewer than 30 enforced policies are exempt.

I could not confirm whether every example plan in this app follows these filters, and the app does not test for them yet.

Limits

  • A rate plan belongs to a network or plan group, not to one named plan. Plan names appear only when the insurer includes them in the plan header.
  • Medians pool all providers in a plan. The same service can look cheaper in one plan only because that plan has different providers.
  • Some plans (Select Health, Aetna) are single provider contracts or subsets, so their coverage numbers are not comparable to a full network plan.
  • Allowed values in the schema checks were written from memory of the CMS schema and should be confirmed against the published schema before a report is released.
  • Insurer links expire or move. Cigna's links are signed and expire around mid 2027.
